Breadth-first search (BFS) is an algorithm used to traverse a [[id:B5F5D7D1-C376-473B-B89F-0E05AE6F5492][graph]] layer-by-layer. Explore outwards from $s$ in all directions simultaneously. The algorithm computes a sequence of layers:

\begin{aligned}
L_0 & = \left\{s\right\} \\
 L_1 & = \text{all neighbors of} \; s \\
 L_2 & = \text{all neighbors of} \; L_1 \; \text{that are nto already in} \; L_0 \; \text{or} \; L_1 \\
 L_{i + 1} & = \text{all vertices having a neighbor in} \; L_i \; \text{that are not already in} \; L_0 , \ldots , L_i
\end{aligned}

Observation: $L_i$ consists of vertices exactly at distance $i$ from $s$. There is a path from $s$ to $t$ if $t$ appears in some layer.

#+begin_quote
Claim: Let $u v$ be an edge of $G$. The layers of $u$ anv $v$ differ by at most one.
+ Assume $u \in L_i$ and $v \in L_j$, where $j \geq i + 2$
+ By definition of $L_j$, $v$ is not in $L_i$ or lower
+ Hence, $v$ must be in $L_i + 1$ - contradiction!
#+end_quote

*** Implementation

#+begin_src cpp
  queue<int> q;
  vector<bool> visited(n);
  q.push(s);
  visited[s] = true;
  while (!q.empty()) {
    int u = q.front();
    q.pop();

    for (int v : adj[u]) {
      if (!visited[v]) {
        visited[v] = true;
        q.push(v);
      }
    }
  }
#+end_src

If we want to keep track of the layers, we may also increment a variable $i$ and keep track of layers as individual lists. Equivalently, we can maintain a =vector<int> dist= instead of =vector<bool> visited=. The BFS algorithm runs in time $O\left(m + n\right)$.
